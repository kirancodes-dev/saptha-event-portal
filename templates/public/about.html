SapthaEvent is the event portal of {{ inst.name }}. It takes a campus event from the first announcement to the last certificate in one place, so that students, organisers and the university's office all look at the same record.

Who it is for

A university or college that runs many events a year through its clubs, departments and student bodies, and wants one portal for all of them instead of a form and a spreadsheet for each.

  • Students and visitors find an event, register, get a ticket and later their certificate.
  • Organisers (club and department coordinators) create events, manage registrations and run the event day.
  • Event-day staff (coordinators and volunteers) check people in and follow the run sheet.
  • Judges score entries for competitions.
  • The university's office approves events, manages accounts and reads the reports.

What it covers

Publishing an event
A step-by-step form for {{ event_type_count }} kinds of event, from a guest lecture to a multi-day fest with its own programme. An event can be sent for approval before it is published.
Registration
Individual or team registration with the organiser's own form questions, a seat limit and a waitlist. An event is for this institution only, open to other institutions, or by invitation. Every page shows the same registration state: open, waitlist, closed, not open yet, over or cancelled.
The event day
QR tickets and a scanner for the gate. If the scanner's connection drops it keeps the scans and the server checks them when the connection returns. A schedule with rooms, people responsible and a status for each item; a room booked twice is refused when the schedule is saved; a change of time or room can be announced to the people registered.
Judging and results
Judges score against the organiser's criteria; results and a leaderboard are published when the organiser decides.
Feedback and certificates
Attendees rate the event. Certificates are issued with a number that anyone can check on the verification page.
Reports
For each event and across events: registrations, attendance and no-shows, results, feedback and certificates, with a date range, a statement of what each figure counts, and CSV and Excel downloads.
Campus data
The student roster comes in from a CSV file, with a preview of what will change before anything is saved. Events can be added to a calendar. Sign in with Google is available when the institution sets it up.
Messages
Email and in-portal notifications. WhatsApp messages are sent only where the institution has set that up and the person has agreed.
Personal data
A privacy notice; each person can download their own data and ask for their account to be deleted.

Paid events use the institution's own payment provider account, where it has set one up.

What it does not do

  • One deployment serves one institution. Institutions do not share a deployment, and an institution cannot sign itself up on this site.
  • It does not connect to a learning management system or an ERP. Student details come in from a CSV file.
  • Other than Sign in with Google, there is no campus single sign-on.
  • Its reports are counts from its own records. They have not been checked against the format of any accreditation or regulatory return.
  • It holds no security or privacy certification, has not been independently audited, and comes with no uptime commitment. The status page shows what is running now, not a history.
  • This site has no price list.

How an institution gets started

  1. A deployment of its own. The portal is installed for the institution on a cloud account, with its own database, file storage and mail sender.
  2. Its details. An administrator enters the institution's name, address, support contact and certificate colours on the setup page, which also lists what is still missing before people use it.
  3. Its administrators. At least two people at the institution are given administrator accounts, and the installer's own account is switched off.
  4. Its students. The roster is uploaded from a CSV file, or students create their own accounts when they first register.
  5. Its organisers. Administrators give organiser accounts to club and department coordinators, who then create events.
  6. A first event, run end to end: publish, register, check in, results, certificates, report.

The institution can export its data and close its deployment down at any time; both are written up in the setup guide that comes with the software.

A demo is a separate copy of the portal filled with sample people and events. On a demo you can:

  • browse events and register for one as a visitor;
  • sign in as a student, an organiser, a coordinator or a judge, with the demo accounts given to you by whoever is showing it;
  • create an event, build its schedule, scan a ticket, publish results and issue certificates;
  • open the reports and download them.

A demo cannot show:

  • your institution's name, students, departments or events: those exist only on a deployment of your own;
  • anything lasting: sample data is emptied and rebuilt, so do not put real personal details into a demo;
  • messages that this copy has no provider for: email, WhatsApp and online payment each work only when that copy has been given an account for them. The status page shows which are set up, and whether online payment is in the provider's test mode;
  • how the portal behaves under your event's crowd: a demo is not a load test.
